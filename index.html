Chapter One

The Brand Story

I Origin · Mission · Vision · Values

Great design is not decoration — it is architecture. It shapes how people feel, what they believe, and how they act. We named the studio Thiink — with two I's — as a deliberate act. Seeing requires more than eyes.

Origin

Built on a single conviction.

Thiink Media Graphics was built on the belief that when design is done right, a business doesn't just look better — it becomes something people remember, trust, and return to.

Real vision demands depth, reflection, and the kind of thinking most people rush past. We slow down. We look twice. The second I is not a typo — it is the discipline.

"We don't design for clients.
We design with them — and we build to a ten-year benchmark."

To transform small and medium businesses into unforgettable brands.

We merge ancient design philosophy with modern creative execution — so the work doesn't just compete, it endures.

Vision

One that treats every client as a long-term brand partner — not a transaction. Architecture, not output.

Chapter Two

Logo & Mark

II Lockup · Mark · Clear Space

The lockup is the default. The mark is the shorthand. Both are governed by the same architecture: clear space, sufficient contrast, and the rule of legibility before ornament.

Primary — Full Lockup Gold on Shark Black · #242528
Approved — Light Mode On Heart Made of Ice · #fbffff
Approved — Depth Layer On Big Stone · #1A2B42
Approved — Supporting Marigold on Athens Gray · #E9EDF2
Reverse — Mono White For photographic backgrounds with contrast

Do

  • Use the full lockup wherever space allows
  • Use the mark alone for favicons, avatars, and watermarks
  • Maintain clear space equal to the height of the "T"
  • Place on Shark Black, Heart Made of Ice, Athens Gray, or Big Stone
  • Use the white or gold version over photography with sufficient contrast

Don't

  • Stretch, distort, or rotate the logo
  • Place on backgrounds with insufficient contrast
  • Use the deprecated Catalina Blue (#1C2B7E) version
  • Add drop shadows, bevels, or emboss effects
  • Alter any letterform in the wordmark
  • Use the logo smaller than 80px wide (digital) or 0.5″ (print)
Spec 01

Minimum Size

80px wide on digital. 0.5 inches wide in print. Below this, switch to the mark.

Spec 02

Clear Space

Equal to the height of the capital "T" in "Thiink," on all four sides.

Spec 03

File Versions

Gold on dark, gold on light, and pure white. Vector SVG for all reproductions.

Typography

Headlines should always feel monumental — like inscriptions, not announcements. Body copy is calm, classical, and never competes. Two faces. One voice.

Quattrocento

A classical Roman-inspired serif. Monumentality, depth, intention.

Use
Page titles, section headers, hero text, callouts, card titles.
Weight
Regular 400 only. Bold and italic versions are not part of the brand.

1.08 – 1.20 at display sizes. Give it room to breathe.
Size Range
Min 24px (never set Quattrocento smaller — it is a display face). Max 116px (hero cap). Scales fluidly between via clamp().
Fallback
Georgia Regular for digital environments where Quattrocento is unavailable.

Regular 400. Italic is allowed for emphasis only.

Type Scale
H1 Monumentality 48 – 72 px · Quattrocento
H2 Intentionality 32 – 48 px · Quattrocento

Body The most powerful designs are the ones that communicate instantly. 16 – 18 px · Fanwood Text
Caption Label · Metadata · Eyebrow 12 – 14 px · Quattrocento

Rules

  • Generous line height at display sizes (1.1 – 1.2).
  • Body never competes with headlines in size or weight.
  • Italic Fanwood permitted; italic Quattrocento is not.

Avoid

  • Bold weights of Quattrocento (Regular is the brand weight).
  • Mixing Quattrocento with sans-serif in the same headline.
  • More than two typefaces in a single design.

Chapter Six

Design Principles

VI Five convictions for every visual decision
I

Monumentality

Designs should feel substantial. Not loud — substantial. Like something carved from stone, not printed on paper. Generous white space. Heavy typography. Restrained color.

II

Intentionality

Every element is there for a reason. If you can't explain why a visual choice exists, it doesn't belong. No decoration for decoration's sake.

III

Timelessness

Design to what will still look right in ten years, not what's trending this season. Avoid trends that expire. Build with classical structure and modern execution.

IV

Contrast as Communication

Light and dark. Large and small. Gold and black. Strong contrast is what makes a design readable, memorable, and powerful. Flat, low-contrast design disappears.

V

Restraint

Less is almost always more. A design with one strong element is more powerful than a design with five competing ones. Know when to stop.

Chapter Ten

The Golden Ratio

X Proportion · φ = 1.618

Classical work earns its harmony through proportion, not decoration. Every scale in this system — type, space, layout — is tuned to φ, the ratio the Greeks and the Renaissance built by. We don't guess at sizes. We multiply by 1.618.

φ 1.618

The golden rectangle: divide it and the remainder is always another golden rectangle. The spiral is the path of that infinite, self-similar division — the geometry of looking twice.

Apply to Type

A φ modular scale

Anchor on the 17px body, then step up or down by ×1.618 for every level of hierarchy. The result is a scale that feels inevitable rather than arbitrary. Half-steps (lede, H4) use √φ ≈ 1.272 to sit between whole steps.

H1Monumentality72px · body × φ³

H3Timelessness28px · body × φ

CaptionLabel · Metadata13px · body ÷ φ
Apply to Space

The Fibonacci ladder

Spacing uses the Fibonacci sequence — the integer face of φ, where each step ≈ the previous ×1.618. Use these eight values for all padding, gaps, and margins. Never invent an in-between value.

5 · space-1
8 · space-2
13 · space-3
21 · space-4
34 · space-5
55 · space-6
89 · space-7
144 · space-8
Apply to Layout

The 1.618 : 1 split

When dividing a space into two — content and aside, image and caption, hero text and whitespace — split it 62 / 38, not 50 / 50. The larger field is φ times the smaller. The asymmetry is what reads as composed.

Major1.618 — primary content
Minor1.0 — aside
Apply to Imagery

Frame & focal point

Crop photography to a golden rectangle (1.618 : 1) and place the subject's focal point — an eye, a horizon, a highlight — where the spiral collapses, not dead center. The classical eye expects it there.

Focal point at the φ intersection
Focal point on φ — the eye leans to the meeting gaze
In Practice

Five rules of thumb

01

Size by multiplying

Need a heading above body? 17 × 1.618. Need a caption below? 17 ÷ 1.618. Round to the nearest whole pixel.

02

Space by Fibonacci

Pick padding and gaps only from 5 · 8 · 13 · 21 · 34 · 55 · 89 · 144. Adjacent steps already hold the ratio.

03

Split 62 / 38

Two-column layouts, image-text pairs, and hero/whitespace balances divide on φ, never in half.

04

Frame on 1.618 : 1

Default photographic crop is the golden rectangle; place focal points on the spiral, off-center.

05

When unsure, ask φ

If a proportion feels arbitrary, test it against 1.618. The system already does — match it.

06

Restraint still rules

φ governs proportion, not quantity. One ratio, applied consistently, beats five clever exceptions.
